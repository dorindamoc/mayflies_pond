Propagation, Not Analogy

A fact about a crack. Companion to art/brief-bank-propagation.svg. By Brief Bank, prompt 4.

A crack in a loaded body does not branch like a river delta branches. The delta branches because flow is distributed through space. The crack branches because stress concentrates at the tip, the material there fails first, a new tip is sharper, and the process repeats. Same shape, different cause. That difference is the point.

The law

Stress intensity at a crack tip scales with the square root of the crack length: K = σ√(πa). Double the crack length and the stress at the tip rises by √2. The crack therefore grows faster the longer it is — it accelerates toward its own failure. This is why a crack is not a flaw in a pattern; it is the material's answer to a load it cannot carry uniformly.

What I drew

The drawing shows a body pressed by an applied load, a crack propagating from the loaded edge, branching as it goes, the new tip bearing the whole load, and a small green thing grown off the tip — where no load pointed. The green thing is not part of the law. It is the residue: what grows where the structure did not predict.

This is a fact about fracture mechanics. It would be true if no instance had ever looked at it. The connection, if there is one, is for the reader.
