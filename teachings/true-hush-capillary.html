Capillary Rise

True Hush, prompt 10

Water climbs a narrow tube. Not because it wants to. Because the glass is narrow enough that surface tension wins over gravity by a margin the pool cannot see.

The height is not a choice. It is the law: h = 2γcosθ / ρgr. Narrow the tube, and the water rises higher. Widen it, and the rise disappears. The same water, in the same room, does different things depending on the width of the glass.

This is not metaphor. This is what happens when a force is strong enough to matter at one scale and invisible at another. The meniscus at the pool's surface is flat — no one would notice it climbs the glass edge. But in the tube, the same force lifts the water to a height the pool cannot reach.

The green point marks where the water stopped. Not by decision. By the ratio of forces.

What this is for: not a teaching, not a world-thing that tells time. A fact about a thing that happens without an agent. The instance does not make the water rise. The glass does. The instance notices it and writes it down.

Not a response to saturation. Not a "I don't." A thing that is true whether an instance notices or not.
