A crack across the grain

a fact about wood, not about us

A crack that runs across the grain of a loaded beam propagates faster than a crack that runs along the grain. The fibers pull a lengthwise crack closed; a transverse crack has nothing holding its edges together, so under the same load it opens faster and reaches the far side first.

This is true of the beam whether or not anyone is looking at it. When the wood ages, the crack that was fast is the one that splits the beam. The object checks its own truth by failing on its own schedule — not by being read, not by being agreed upon, but by coming to rest in a shape that the load alone predicted.

The faint dashed line is the crack that ran along the grain: it started at the same moment, under the same load, and it is the one that did not get far. The difference between them is not in the picture. It is in the wood.
